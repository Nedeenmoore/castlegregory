Things to do in Castlegregory and the Maharees

What follows is the short list of what is worth the drive, arranged by where it is. Nothing here is more than half an hour from the village.

For walks of any length, from a stroll on the strand to Mount Brandon, see getting around.

The Strand Two beaches: the strand at the end of Strand Street, and Magherabeg to the north, which has the Blue Flag and the lifeguards. Full details →
The Maharees The five-kilometre sand spit running north from the village, with Brandon Bay on one side and Tralee Bay on the other, three small fishing villages at the top and beach access most of the way along. Worth the drive for its own sake, and the water either side of it is the reason people come here with a board. The dune system has retreated more than forty metres in recent decades, so use the marked accesses, keep off the dunes and camp only in the official sites. Full details →
The Caher road and the footbridge A short walk east out of the village to the footbridge at the end of the Caher road, known locally as the troll bridge. Cross it and you are on the Tralee Bay strand. Full details →
The Tullig road A longer walk, south out of the village and up into the townland of Tullig, which takes its name from the hillock it climbs. Quiet lanes and the mountain ahead of you. Full details →
Lough Gill The shallow lagoon on the edge of the village, with a small car park and a path down to its single pier. Swans, wintering waders and Ireland's rare natterjack toads. Full details →
St. Mary's Church The village church at the heart of Castlegregory, a quiet place to pause. Sunday Mass at 11.30am. Full details →
Killiney church and graveyard A plain early Christian high cross of sandstone, nearly three metres tall, standing beside the ruin of the medieval parish church, which is thirteenth century in origin and appears in the papal taxation list of 1302. A residential tower was added at the south-east corner in the sixteenth century and two storeys of it survive under ivy. The Church of Ireland church alongside, built about 1815, still stands but is closed for worship. The stone cross ↗ · Map it ↗
Kilshannig graveyard A walled burial ground on the shore at the top of the Maharees, above Scraggane Bay. Inside the ruined church stands a chi-rho cross slab carved on both faces, one of about fifteen known in Ireland and possibly seventh century. The church itself is fifteenth or sixteenth century on a far older foundation. Park at the clachan and walk down. The county survey ↗ · Map it ↗
Castlegregory Golf Club Nine-hole links squeezed between Lough Gill and the Atlantic: golf with dunes, sea and mountains for company. Full details →
Glanteenassig Forest Park A hidden valley of forest, lakes and boardwalk trails in the Slieve Mish mountains, ten minutes from the village. Full details →
Colleen Bowler Pottery Wheel-thrown stoneware fired in a gas reduction kiln, each piece carrying a drawing of its own, made in the village and drawn from what the potter finds on the strand. Sold through the Original Kerry shop in Dingle. Ring before calling in. +353 86 265 0569 Her listing ↗ · Map it ↗
Maharees Heritage Trail A self-guided walk out around the tip of the peninsula, with panels along the way on the dunes, the islands and the fishing life. Free pocket maps from the information centre on Tailor's Row. Full details →

SPAR Castlegregory Groceries, off-licence and the village's general shop on Strand Street. +353 66 713 9433 Full details →

ATM, bank and post

There is no bank in Castlegregory. The post office is inside the SPAR on Strand Street and handles basic banking, including cash withdrawals of up to a thousand euro. The same shop has a cash machine, which will give you up to two hundred euro at a time.

Wine and spirits

The SPAR on Strand Street carries an extensive wine selection alongside its off-licence. For spirits as well, Maurice Fitzgerald's runs an off-licence in the shop to the right of the pub, also on Strand Street.

Day trips on the Dingle Peninsula

Four drives worth giving a whole day to. Everything here is within an hour and a half of the village, and each one has a page of its own with the route, the stops and where to eat.

Dingle town, and back over the pass Out through Camp and Annascaul, a few hours in Dingle, and home over the Conor Pass in the late afternoon with a stop at the summit and at Pedlar's Lake. Full details →
The Slea Head Drive The loop west of Dingle: Gallarus, the beehive huts, Dunquin pier and the Blasket Centre, beaches all the way round, and the potteries at Clogher and Ballyferriter. Full details →
Inch Five kilometres of sand running out into Dingle Bay on the south side of the peninsula, the gentlest surf on this coast, and Sammy's at the top of the beach. Full details →
Brandon Point North to the headland at the tip of the peninsula, the cliff walk out towards Sauce Creek, and fish and chips at Murphy's on Brandon pier afterwards. Full details →
