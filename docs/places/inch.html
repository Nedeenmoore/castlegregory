A day out

Inch

Inch is on the other side of the peninsula, a spit of sand running out into Dingle Bay off the R561 between Annascaul and Castlemaine, with a dune system behind it that forms part of the Castlemaine Harbour Special Area of Conservation. It is roughly five kilometres long, it holds the Blue Flag, and David Lean filmed part of Ryan's Daughter on it.

It is the gentlest surf on this end of Kerry and the best place to learn. Two approved schools work the beach: Kingdom Waves, on 087 744 7958 from April to November, and Offshore Surf School on 087 294 6519, who run year round out of orange trailers on the sand and hire boards, wetsuits, bodyboards and paddleboards.

Sammy's sits at the top of the beach and has been in the same family since 1987: shop, café, restaurant and bar in one, with oysters from Dingle Bay, the catch of the day, and hake for the fish and chips. Hours move with the season, so ring on 066 716 5013.

Good to know

Driving on the sand. People do it and it is tolerated in the designated area, but Kerry County Council's beach bye-laws make it subject to the council's consent, and the tide at Inch comes in fast. In August 2022 two vehicles were caught by it and a great many more were bogged in soft sand. Check the tide before you drive on, stay well back from the water, and do not take a low car far down the beach.

The car park across the road is privately owned and has been the subject of a dispute since the council's lease ran out at the end of 2025. It was free for the summer of 2026 after a last-minute agreement. Take coins.
